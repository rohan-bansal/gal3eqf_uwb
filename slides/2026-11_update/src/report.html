1Motivation

We localize a drone with an IMU and UWB. A tag on the drone sends packets and each anchor records the arrival time. Instead of subtracting arrival times (TDoA), which removes the tag clock, we keep the raw timestamps and estimate the tag clock's offset and skew with the pose. Clock offset and skew, together with rotation, velocity and position, form the Galilean group Gal(3). Delama et al. (RSS 2026) built an equivariant filter (EqF) on \(\mathrm{Gal}(3)\ltimes\mathfrak{gal}(3)\) for GNSS and IMU. We apply it to UWB and ask three questions:

  1. Q1. Does the coupling between clock and motion in Gal(3) matter for UWB? The coupling grows with speed, so we test speeds up to 45 m/s.
  2. Q2. Is the EqF more consistent than an EKF, and when? We test it with harder clock setups, ending with unsynchronized anchors that send packets to each other (anchor packets). The distance between two anchors is fixed, so these packets measure the anchor clocks.
  3. Q3. If the anchor positions are not surveyed, does the EqF avoid the overconfidence of EKF-SLAM? The filter then estimates the anchor positions in flight. Rotating or shifting the whole scene does not change any measurement, and EKFs are known to become overconfident in these directions. Our EqF includes the anchors in the symmetry group, so these directions stay fixed in its error.

2Problem definition

Let \(T\) be the tag's clock time, which also stamps the IMU, and \(t\) the anchor network's time. The state is

\[ x = (R,\ v,\ p,\ \delta,\ b_\omega,\ b_a,\ b_\rho),\qquad \delta \triangleq t - T, \]

with attitude \(R\in SO(3)\), world-frame velocity and position \(v,p\in\mathbb R^3\), tag clock offset \(\delta\), gyro and accelerometer biases \(b_\omega, b_a\), and tag clock skew \(b_\rho\). With gyro and accelerometer readings \(\omega_m, a_m\), the dynamics in tag time are

\[ \dot R = R\,(\omega_m - b_\omega)^\wedge,\quad \dot v = R\,(a_m - b_a) + g,\quad \dot p = v,\quad \dot\delta = -b_\rho,\quad \dot b = \eta_b, \tag{1} \]

with white noise on the readings and random walks \(\eta_b\) on the biases. Terms of relative size \(b_\rho \approx 2\times10^{-5}\) are dropped from (1); the Galilean model (6) keeps them.

Tag packets. A packet sent at tag time \(T_k\) reaches anchor \(i\) at network time \(t_{k,i}\). In metres,

\[ y_{k,i} = c\,(t_{k,i} - T_k) = \lVert p + R\,\ell - a_i \rVert + c\,(\delta + \Delta_i) + n_{k,i}, \tag{2} \]

with noise \(n_{k,i}\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\), lever arm \(\ell\), anchor positions \(a_i\), and anchor clock offsets \(\Delta_i\) relative to anchor 0 (zero when the anchors are synchronized). Differencing two rows gives TDoA and removes \(\delta\); we keep all \(N\) rows and estimate \(\delta\).

Anchor packets. Unsynchronized anchors add offsets and skews with \(\dot\Delta_i = \kappa_i\) and \(\Delta_0 \equiv 0\), since anchor 0 defines network time. They are observable through packets that anchor \(j\) sends and anchor \(m\) timestamps,

\[ y_{jm} = \lVert a_m - a_j \rVert + c\,(\Delta_m - \Delta_j) + n. \tag{3} \]

Unsurveyed anchors. If the \(a_i\) are unknown they join the state with a prior \(\hat a_i^{(0)} \sim \mathcal N(a_i, \sigma_a^2 I)\). For any rotation \(R_z\) about gravity and translation \(\tau\in\mathbb R^3\), the scene motion

\[ (R,\ v,\ p,\ a_i) \mapsto (R_z R,\ R_z v,\ R_z p + \tau,\ R_z a_i + \tau) \tag{4} \]

leaves (1)–(3) unchanged. These four directions are unobservable and only the priors fix them, so a consistent filter must not gain information along them.

Figure 1 shows the four settings we test, each adding one difficulty to the last (details in section 5).

Goal. Estimate \(x\) recursively with a covariance \(P\) that matches the actual error \(e\), \(\mathbb E[ee^\top] = P\). We measure the match by the normalized estimation error squared per degree of freedom,

\[ \overline{\mathrm{NEES}} = \frac{1}{\dim e}\, e^\top P^{-1} e, \tag{5} \]

averaged over time and Monte Carlo runs: 1 is consistent, above 1 is overconfident.

3Filter

3.1State on the Galilean group

A Galilean transformation acts on events as \((x, t) \mapsto (Rx + vt + p,\ t + s)\). The drone's body frame and tag clock, relative to the world and the network clock, form one such transformation:

\[ F = \begin{bmatrix} R & v_F & p_F \\ 0 & 1 & \delta \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \in \mathrm{Gal}(3),\qquad v = v_F + g\,\delta,\quad p = p_F + \tfrac12 g\,\delta^2, \]

where \(v_F, p_F\) belong to the free-falling frame. The biases fill the four slots of the Lie algebra, \(b = (b_\omega, b_a, b_\nu, b_\rho) \in \mathfrak{gal}(3)\), and so does the IMU input, \(w = (\omega_m, a_m, 0, 1)\). The velocity-input bias \(b_\nu\) is zero in the true system and is needed for the symmetry. The system is

\[ \dot F = -g_N^\wedge F + F\,(w - b)^\wedge,\qquad \dot b = \eta_b,\qquad g_N = (0,\ {-g},\ 0,\ 1). \tag{6} \]

The time slot gives \(\dot\delta = -b_\rho\): the tag skew is the bias of the time-rate input, as \(b_\omega\) is the bias of the angular-rate input. The space slots give (1) with the skew terms kept, \(\dot p = (1-b_\rho)\,v\) and \(\dot v = R(a_m - b_a) + (1-b_\rho)\,g\).

3.2Symmetry and error

The symmetry group is \(\mathbf G = \mathrm{Gal}(3)\ltimes\mathfrak{gal}(3)\), of dimension 20, with product \((A,\alpha)(B,\beta) = (AB,\ \alpha + \mathrm{Ad}_A\beta)\). The state is identified with \(X = (F,\ {-\mathrm{Ad}_F\, b}) \in \mathbf G\), and the EqF's error and its coordinates are

\[ E = X\hat X^{-1},\qquad X = \exp(\varepsilon)\,\hat X,\qquad \varepsilon \in \mathbb R^{20}. \tag{7} \]

3.3Propagation and update

Propagation. Each IMU sample moves the estimate along (6), and the covariance along the linearized error dynamics:

\[ \begin{gathered} \hat F \leftarrow \Gamma\,\hat F\,\exp\!\big((w - \hat b)\,\Delta T\big),\qquad \Gamma = \exp(-g_N \Delta T), \\ P \leftarrow A P A^\top + B Q B^\top. \end{gathered} \tag{8} \]

The block of \(A\) that acts on the navigation error is \(\mathrm{Ad}_\Gamma\), which depends only on \(g\) and \(\Delta T\), not on the estimate. The EKF's transition matrix contains \(\hat R\), \(\hat v\) and the measured acceleration.

Update. A tag packet gives the residual \(r = y - h(\hat X)\), with \(h\) from (2). With \(u_i\) the unit vector from anchor \(i\) to the estimated tag, the row of \(H\) for anchor \(i\) is

\[ H_i = \big[\, -u_i^\top (\hat p_F + \hat R\ell)^\wedge \quad \hat\delta\, u_i^\top \quad u_i^\top \quad c + \hat\delta\, u_i^\top g \;\big|\; 0_{1\times 10} \,\big] \tag{9} \]

over the rotation, boost, translation and time coordinates of \(\varepsilon\), and zero over the biases. With the usual Kalman gain \(K\), the correction is applied on the group, \(\hat X \leftarrow \exp(Kr)\,\hat X\), and \(P\) is then re-expressed around the new estimate.

The \(\hat\delta\) entries of (9) are the Galilean coupling between clock and motion. UWB measures \(\delta\) to about 0.1 ns, and in that time a drone at 45 m/s moves 4.5 nm. So we expect no gain on the clock (Q1). Any advantage of the EqF has to come from how it couples rotation, velocity and position.

The filter above is the one used in S1. Each later scenario adds noise, states or measurements. Select a scenario to see what changes.

4Baselines

4.1EKF

An error-state EKF on (1), in the style of Shalaby et al. and Delama et al.'s "EKF (online)", with the same inputs, noise and initial estimate as the EqF. Its error is

\[ e = \big(\mathrm{Log}(\hat R^\top R),\ v - \hat v,\ p - \hat p,\ \delta - \hat\delta,\ b - \hat b\big) \in \mathbb R^{17}, \tag{10} \]

propagated with the Jacobian of (1) at \(\hat x\) and reset after each update. The EKF has no \(b_\nu\), so it has 17 states where the EqF has 20. In the EKF the anchor clocks (S3) and anchor positions (S4) are added to \(e\) as additive errors. Otherwise the two filters differ only in the error, (7) against (10).

4.2Why the EKF is overconfident with unsurveyed anchors

In the EKF's coordinates the scene rotation (4) is the direction

\[ N_{\mathrm{yaw}}(\hat x) = \big(\hat R^\top e_z,\ e_z\times\hat v,\ e_z\times\hat p,\ 0,\ e_z\times\hat a_0,\ \dots,\ e_z\times\hat a_{N-1}\big), \tag{11} \]

which depends on the estimate. In the EqF it is the constant \((e_z, 0, \dots, 0)\) (section 3.4, S4). A linearized filter keeps \(N\) unobservable if \(H_k N_k = 0\) and \(\Phi_k N_k = N_{k+1}\), with each \(N_k\) evaluated where \(H_k\) and \(\Phi_k\) are. The EKF evaluates \(H_k\) at \(\hat x_k^-\) but propagates from \(\hat x_k^+\), so the second condition fails at every update. The EKF then gains information about the heading of the whole scene, which no measurement provides.

4.3FEJ-EKF

The first-estimates Jacobian EKF (Huang, Mourikis and Roumeliotis, ICRA 2008) is a common fix. It evaluates every Jacobian at points that do not change after an update: each anchor at its first estimate \(\hat a_i^{(0)}\), and the drone at its propagated estimate \(\hat x_k^-\). The step out of an update uses the closed-form transition of Li and Mourikis (2013), which maps \(N(\hat x_k^-)\) exactly to \(N(\hat x_{k+1}^-)\). The cost is that the anchor Jacobians stay at the initial guess for the whole flight, about 30 cm off when \(\sigma_a = 30\) cm. The EqF gets the correct unobservable directions with Jacobians at the current estimate. We run FEJ-EKF only in S4.

6Results

6.1Surveyed anchors (S1 to S3)

  • Accuracy is identical. Position error is 3.0 to 3.2 cm at low speed and 3.5 to 3.8 cm at 25 to 45 m/s; the filters never differ by more than 0.01 cm.
  • The EqF is more consistent only near hover. At hover its NEES is 1.06, 1.08 and 1.02 in S1 to S3, against the EKF's 1.16, 1.19 and 1.07, mostly from heading (1.23 against 1.31 to 1.41). From a poor start (30 cm, uncalibrated 0.01 rad/s gyro bias) the EKF reaches 1.8 at hover and the EqF 1.2. From 5 to 45 m/s the two agree within 0.02.
  • Q1: the clock coupling has no measurable effect. The tag clock error stays at 0.5, 3.0 and 4.2 cm of light travel in S1 to S3, the same at 45 m/s as at hover, for both filters.

6.2Real IMU (MILUV, S1 to S3)

The two filters give the same results on every flight: horizontal error 3.3 to 6.1 cm, vertical 7 to 62 cm for both because of the coplanar anchors. In S1 the no-drift clock model absorbs real-IMU errors into \(\delta\), and both filters are badly overconfident on some flights. With a drifting clock (S2, S3) NEES is 0.8 to 5.0 and equal on all but one flight.

6.3Unsurveyed anchors (S4)

With a 30 cm survey:

  • S1 and S2, 2 to 5 m/s: the EKF's NEES climbs to 3 to 7 by 90 s and FEJ levels off at 1.7 to 2.5. Only the EqF stays consistent in S1 at 5 m/s (1.05); in S2 it drifts to 2.7.
  • S3: FEJ is the worst (3.7 to 7.6 at 90 s). Its anchor Jacobians are evaluated at the initial guess, 30 cm off, and the anchor packets are applied through them. The EqF stays at 1.04 to 1.14 and the EKF at 1.1 to 1.5.
  • Hover to 2 m/s (S1, S2): no filter is consistent; at hover in S1 the EKF reaches 17.8, FEJ 13.6 and the EqF 28.6. The direction that rotates only the drone, not the scene, is not one of the directions (4), so neither the EqF nor FEJ handles it.
  • Accuracy is equal: drone 12 to 15 cm and anchors 15 to 21 cm, against 2.7 to 2.9 cm surveyed. A 10 cm survey keeps all three at 0.96 to 1.15 whenever the drone moves.
  • MILUV: FEJ is the most robust in S2 (12 to 18 cm, against up to 80 cm for the EKF and 50 cm for the EqF), the most overconfident in S3, and diverged in 2 of 80 S1 runs.

7Conclusions

  • Q1. For UWB the Galilean coupling between clock and motion is nanometres at any drone speed, and the clock estimate is the same for both filters.
  • Q2. With surveyed anchors the EqF matches the EKF's accuracy everywhere and is more consistent only near hover or after a poor start.
  • Q3. With the anchors in the group, the scene directions (4) are exactly unobservable without freezing any linearization point. The EqF is then the only consistent filter at 5 m/s with a fixed tag clock, and stays near 1 with anchor packets from 2 m/s up, where FEJ fails. FEJ is better with a drifting tag clock and in gentle flight, and no filter is consistent at hover.

Limitations. The high-speed scenes are scaled, with anchors up to 227 m high. MILUV's timestamps are simulated. Our FEJ is the standard version; refreshed first estimates or an observability-constrained EKF are untested.
